Model specification · season-strength-v1

A ranking you can reproduce.

The objective is to estimate each team’s current strength from its current-season results. Each league has its own ratings; NBA points, NFL points and NHL goals are separate units.

This is a working research baseline. The parameters below are design choices, not empirically optimized values. No historical predictive accuracy or profitable betting edge has been established. The site labels its matchup calculations as research projections and does not count them as locked picks.

1. What do established approaches measure?

ApproachMain informationUseful interpretation / limitation
Win–loss standingsResultsAchievement so far. Teams can face very different schedules.

Sport-specific efficiency modelsPossession efficiency, play efficiency, or shot qualityPotentially richer signals, with more data and modeling requirements.
Performance against the spreadOutcome relative to a particular market lineMeasures performance against market expectations. It is a different target from absolute team strength.

Sports Reference describes SRS as margin adjusted for opponent difficulty. Our recency weights, ridge penalties and capped margins make this a custom relative-strength model, not an exact reproduction of their SRS. Sports Reference’s explanation.

FiveThirtyEight’s published NFL Elo method illustrates a sequential alternative, including contextual adjustments and a probability mapping. Its historic methodology is a research reference, not a claim about a current prediction service. NFL Elo methodology.

NBA efficiency can be normalized by possessions; NFL play-level EPA measures changes in expected scoring value; NHL expected-goal models assign goal probabilities to shots. These are distinct modeling tasks. None of these richer statistics is an input to this prototype. NBA statistics FAQ, nflfastR data definitions, MoneyPuck methodology.

2. Exactly which data are retrieved?

For each of NBA, NFL and NHL, the Python job uses these ESPN feeds, with the league-specific path:

Base: https://site.api.espn.com/apis/site/v2/sports/{path}
NBA path: basketball/nba
NFL path: football/nfl
NHL path: hockey/nhl

GET /scoreboard
GET /teams?limit=1000
GET /teams/{team_id}/schedule?season={provider_year}&seasontype=2
GET /teams/{team_id}/schedule?season={provider_year}&seasontype=3
    (playoffs only after the provider reports the playoff/offseason phase)

Pick verification / grading only:
GET /summary?event={game_id}

The scoreboard identifies the provider’s current season. The team directory supplies stable IDs, names and abbreviations. Each team schedule supplies game ID, season, phase, scheduled time, home and away IDs, neutral-site flag, completion status and final score. There is one shared game per event ID, even though it appears in both teams’ schedules. Conflicting duplicates, unrecognized teams, incomplete team coverage, invalid scores or missing venue flags stop the refresh.

The rating fit uses only final regular-season and, when applicable, playoff games from that same season with scheduled starts before the snapshot. Preseason, live games, future games and earlier seasons are excluded. NFL season identifiers and NBA/NHL season identifiers follow ESPN’s metadata; they are not guessed from the calendar year.

The snapshot records its calculation time and observation time. Dates are stored with timezones and displayed in America/Toronto. The job archives the normalized source games, parameters and an SHA-256 digest of its fitted inputs in rankings.json. The hash detects a mismatch against those archived inputs; the hash alone is not independent proof of when they existed.

These public feeds can change or fail. A refresh failure leaves the previous published snapshot in place, with its original displayed update time. The job does not replace missing games with invented results. The inputs archive is the complete normalized model input, rather than a copy of every field in ESPN’s raw response.

3. The equations

For game g, let yg be the final home score minus the final away score. Let ag be the number of days between its scheduled start and the snapshot. The venue flag vg is 0 for a neutral site and 1 otherwise.

y_g = home score − away score
c_g = max(−C, min(C, y_g))
w_g = 2^(−a_g / H)

fitted margin_g = r_home(g) − r_away(g) + h × v_g

(r*, h*) = argmin:
  Σ_g w_g × [c_g − (r_home(g) − r_away(g) + h × v_g)]²
  + λ × Σ_i r_i²
  + λ_h × h²

Opponent adjustment: all team ratings ri are estimated together. The opponent’s rating is in each game equation. It is not an extra subjective bonus added after ranking.

Recency: a game H days old receives half the weight of a new game. A game 2H days old receives one quarter. Every completed current-season game can still contribute.

Blowout cap: a winning margin greater than C receives the same target as a margin of C. The raw margin is still preserved and displayed separately. This reduces the influence of extreme final scores; it also discards some information and needs testing.

Shrinkage: λ penalizes extreme team ratings when evidence is limited. λh similarly shrinks the estimated home advantage toward zero. The home effect is estimated from the same league’s games, rather than borrowed from another sport. With this symmetric team penalty, team ratings sum to zero, apart from rounding.

Matrix solution: each game row of X contains +1 for its home team, −1 for its away team and its venue flag in a final home-advantage column. W is diagonal with the game weights. The penalty matrix D contains λ for every team and λh for the home column.

β = [r_1, r_2, …, r_n, h]ᵀ
β* = (Xᵀ W X + D)⁻¹ Xᵀ W c

The implementation solves the linear system with Gaussian elimination and partial pivoting; it does not explicitly invert a matrix. Positive penalties give a unique solution, including when the early-season schedule is disconnected.

5. Reading the site

Higher ratings mean stronger modeled performance. A +4 rating is four units above the league’s model average in the capped-margin fit. It is not a guaranteed four-point expected winning margin. Rating differences and the fitted home term produce a research rating edge for a scheduled matchup. Turning that edge into a calibrated win probability or spread prediction requires separate evaluation.

home rating edge = r_home − r_away + h × venue

raw margin/game_i = Σ (points scored_i − points allowed_i) / games_i
opponent rating_i = Σ (w_g × r_opponent(g)) / Σ w_g
weighted games_i = Σ w_g
rank movement_i = previous saved rank_i − current rank_i

Weighted games is the sum of weights, not a statistical confidence level or formal effective sample size. Movement compares actual saved snapshots with matching season, model version and parameters; it does not refit yesterday using data first observed today. Teams without eligible finals remain unranked. Exact equal ratings, at six-decimal precision, share a rank.

Final scores include overtime and the league-recorded shootout score. NHL overtime losses are losses in this model’s simple W–L summary. The prototype does not isolate regulation play, remove empty-net goals, adjust for basketball pace, separate offense from defense, or incorporate injuries, starting goalies, quarterbacks, rest or travel.

6. Validation before stronger prediction claims

  1. Use rolling chronological evaluations: predict a day from information available before that day’s games, then advance. Each fitted team rating still uses only that season’s earlier games.
  2. Compare the baseline with simple standings, an Elo baseline and an unweighted margin model. Test capped versus uncapped margins, recency windows, shrinkage and venue effects.
  3. Select settings in an earlier validation window; reserve later games for an untouched evaluation. Avoid choosing parameters using the same results reported as final accuracy.
  4. Evaluate raw-margin error separately from winner accuracy. If probabilities are added, evaluate calibration, Brier score and log loss. The prototype’s weighted training RMSE is an in-sample diagnostic, not a forecast-accuracy result.
  5. Evaluate NBA possession-adjusted efficiency, NFL EPA and NHL shot-quality signals using verified data access. More complex inputs should earn their place through held-out results.
  6. Archive model versions and prospective projections. Historical ESPN corrections and final-status timing cannot be reconstructed perfectly from a newly fetched schedule; genuinely prospective snapshots provide stronger evidence.

7. Personal picks and the public record

Your personal picks and the existing blowout system picks are separate records. Personal picks can come from any scheduled NBA, NFL or NHL game, regardless of whether it appeared in the morning blowout report. The new power model’s displayed research projections do not silently become system bets.

The site prepares a JSON draft. You submit it through the authenticated private repository’s GitHub workflow. The backend re-fetches the game, verifies its IDs and scheduled status, and publishes a predictions.json attachment in an immutable release in the public record repository. The server’s actual published_at must be at least 30 minutes before the earliest scheduled leg. Five additional minutes are required during preparation. The browser’s clock and an entered date never establish eligibility.

Automatic grading supports full-game two-way moneylines, spreads/puck lines, game totals and team totals. Other periods, player props, parlays and custom markets require exact locked settlement rules and an owner-reviewed settlement release with a public evidence URL. Their results are visibly marked owner reviewed. A parlay is never treated as a win by guessing how its bookmaker handles a void leg.

Pushes, pending, void and late/ineligible picks remain visible but do not count as wins or losses. No stakes, profit or ROI are tracked. Visitors can inspect the original selection, line, rules, publication timestamp, game result and grading method. A record’s win rate across mixed markets is not by itself a measure of betting quality.

Immutable releases prevent editing their published attachments. GitHub still allows an owner to delete an entire release. This provides an auditable public record, not an indestructible independent archive. The HTML and aggregate JSON are reproducible summaries, rather than the source of truth for what was selected before a game.
